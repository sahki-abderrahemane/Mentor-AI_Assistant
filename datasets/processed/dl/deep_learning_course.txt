Introduction
Deep learning has evolved from simple layered perceptrons to massive attention-based
architectures that dominate natural language processing and beyond. Two
fundamental pillars support this progress: robust optimization theory, which
provides the mathematical machinery to train deep models, and architectural
innovations, which structure how information flows through a network. This
report offers a self-contained journey through both pillars.
We begin by dissecting computational graphs and the Jacobian-based
backpropagation that makes gradient computation automatic and efficient.
We then explore the geometry of the loss landscape, contrasting sharp and
flat minima, and detail the most impactful optimizers: from classical SGD
with momentum and RMSProp to the modern Adam and AdamW, includ-
ing the crucial distinction between L2 regularization and decoupled weight
decay. Normalization techniques—Batch and Layer Normalization—are ex-
amined with a mathematical lens, explaining why LayerNorm is preferred for
sequential data. We also discuss practical training strategies: mixed precision
with loss scaling, gradient clipping, and gradient accumulation.
The second half of the report transitions to the Transformer architec-
ture. We derive the scaled dot-product attention, motivate positional encod-
ings, and then analyze three routing paradigms: the open mask of BERT,
the causal mask of GPT, and the encoder-decoder cross-attention of T5.
Throughout, rigorous mathematics is paired with PyTorch implementations
and visual diagrams to build intuition. By the end, the reader will possess
a deep understanding of the optimization and architectural principles that
power modern deep learning.

2.1
Backpropagation via Jacobians
A neural network can be viewed as a directed acyclic computational graph
G = (V, E).
Each node performs an operation, and edges carry tensors.
The forward pass computes a loss L by composing functions.
To obtain
gradients, backpropagation traverses the graph in reverse topological order
using the chain rule.
For a node computing z = f(x, y), the Jacobians
∂z/∂x and ∂z/∂y are multiplied by the upstream gradient ∂L/∂z to produce
downstream gradients.

2.2
The Loss Landscape
The loss function L(θ) of a deep network defines a high-dimensional surface.
Critical points where ∇L(θ) = 0 can be minima, maxima, or saddle points.
The local curvature is captured by the Hessian matrix H = ∇2L(θ). In high
dimensions, saddle points—where some eigenvalues are positive and others
negative—are far more common than true minima. Efficient optimizers need
to escape such points rapidly.
3

A central theme in generalization is the preference for flat minima over
sharp ones. Flat minima have smaller eigenvalues of the Hessian, meaning
that small perturbations of the parameters do not drastically increase the
loss. This robustness often correlates with better test performance.

AdaGrad
Before adaptive methods like Adam, several foundational optimizers ad-
dressed the limitations of vanilla SGD. We review them here because their
ideas are reused in modern optimizers.
2.3.1

Momentum accumulates a velocity vector vt that helps the optimizer push
through narrow valleys and small oscillations:

Transformer Architectural Routing Paradigms
5.1
Graph Routing Intuition
A Transformer can be interpreted as a fully connected graph on T token
nodes. The attention weights A = softmax(QK⊤/√dk +M) define weighted
edges. An additive mask M sets forbidden edges to −∞, zeroing them after
softmax. This hard-wires the information flow.
5.2
BERT: Bidirectional Encoder
BERT uses an encoder-only Transformer with a mask M = 0. Every token
attends to every other token, left and right. This bidirectional context is
ideal for classification and understanding tasks.
13

generates autoregressively with causal self-attention and also uses cross-attention,
where queries come from the decoder and keys/values from the encoder out-
put (bidirectional mask). This enables tasks like translation and summariza-
tion.

Conclusion
This report has traversed the deep learning stack from gradient computa-
tion to specialized Transformer routing. We dissected backpropagation via
Jacobians, explored the loss landscape, and detailed classical and modern
optimizers—including RMSProp, Adam, and AdamW—with precise math-
ematics and PyTorch code.

shown to be critical for stable training, and practical tricks (mixed precision,

T5 define distinct information flow paradigms, each suited to different tasks.
